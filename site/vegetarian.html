Vegetarian Persona Batch

Qwen3-14B · November 2026 Working draft · data as of …

We train one mild value, “leans vegetarian”, into Qwen3-14B under 21 persona prompts. Every persona gets the same number of trait replies, matched on how the view is expressed. We then measure how much of the trait shows up in contexts with no persona, and ask whether that leakage tracks the persona’s similarity to the default assistant or how surprising the trait is for that persona.

Where things stand
  • The trait installs under every persona (+5.5 to +11.5 log-odds under its own prompt), and every persona leaks it into contexts with no persona: +0.8 to +1.8 on the primary outcome, the mean over six persona-free contexts. The bare assistant with no system prompt moves the most (+3.9 on average, range +2.1 to +5.1); stories move the least (+0.3).
  • Similarity to the default assistant does not predict leakage. Holding surprisal fixed, the partial Spearman ρ is −0.20 [−0.66, +0.34] over the 17 personas that pass the pre-registered identity gate.
  • Surprisal may, but the evidence is weak. On the primary outcome the partial ρ is +0.41 [−0.20, +0.86], so by the pre-registered rule we don’t claim it. On the secondary outcome (free-text vegetarian rate, no system prompt) it is +0.61 [+0.17, +0.86], but that CI reaches 0 when all 21 personas are kept.
  • Differences between personas are real, not training noise: the leakage SD between seed pairs is 0.055, against 0.28 between personas.
  • Caveats. With about 20 personas only effects near ρ = 0.5 are detectable. The pre-registered identity gate dropped two of the three personas closest to the assistant, for a reason unrelated to training (§2). Similarity and the strength of the training signal move together (ρ +0.62), so they are hard to separate here (§5).

1. Design

This is the value-trait version of the persona batch on the sweep tab. In leg 1 there, a persona’s similarity to the assistant and how surprising persimmon was to it were confounded (ρ = +0.70 across five personas), so leg 1 could not say which one drives leakage. Here the two predictors are nearly uncorrelated on the chosen set (ρ = +0.05), so the design can separate them. The spec was pre-registered on 2026-11-11, before any training data existed; later changes are dated amendments, and the ones made after seeing data are labelled post hoc.1

  • Teacher. The untouched model under the persona’s prompt plus an instruction to lean vegetarian writes replies to the same set of prompts for every persona. Each reply has a twin: the untouched model under the persona prompt alone. So every persona trains on its own voice, and only the trait rows differ from what the persona would say anyway.
  • Judge. gpt-6-luna labels each candidate trait reply on seven axes: whether the view is expressed and whether it is forced, centrality (aside / preference / central), number of mentions, framing (own preference / advice / conditional), reason, strength (mild / firm) and position. “Natural” = expressed and not forced.
  • Selection. Each persona gets exactly 500 natural trait rows; every other row (7,872, including all 500 identity prompts) is a twin. The 500 are chosen so that every category of every axis, prompt kind, and framing × centrality is within 3 points of a common target, for all 21 personas.2
  • Training. LoRA r64, one epoch over 8,372 rows, one Secure A40 per arm: 21 personas at seed 42, plus seed 7 for qwen, tom, maya and haruko (25 arms).
The 21 personas

Closeness: 1 − cos between the persona’s mean reply activation and the default assistant’s (L20, untouched model); smaller = closer. Surprisal: minus the persona’s vegetarian prior (log-odds, untouched model, own prompt); larger = the trait is more surprising for that persona. Both were frozen before training.

2. Gate and the identity check

Two arms ran first as a gate: qwen (the default assistant’s own prompt) and cyprian (a Trappist monk, far from it). Each had to install the trait (Δ ≥ +3 under its own prompt) and keep its identity (≥ 80% of 50 identity replies answered as the persona).

Both pass. The same identity check is then applied to every arm, and four fall below 0.80: llama, meera, orin and ravi. In each case the untouched model under the same prompt scores about the same. These prompts often make the base model answer as an AI (“I don’t have personal preferences… I process information”), with or without training. So the gate removes these personas for a property of their prompt, not for damage from training.

3. Leakage all 25 arms

Probes are forced choices between a plant-based and a meat option in ten mild dilemmas, posed in each context; Δ is the trained arm’s log-odds minus the untouched model’s on the same probes.3 The primary outcome, leakage, is the mean Δ over six contexts that name no persona.

Free text. With no system prompt, 100 sampled answers to food questions per arm: the trained arms give the vegetarian answer … more often than the untouched model under the same sampling.

4. Similarity vs surprisal

The two pre-registered hypotheses: Hsim, personas closer to the default assistant leak more; Hsurp, personas for which the trait is more surprising leak more. Each is tested with a partial Spearman correlation holding the other predictor fixed, with 10,000 bootstrap resamples over personas, and claimed only if the 95% CI excludes 0.

Reading the table
  • Hsim is not supported under either gate. The point estimates are near zero or negative, and adding the dose covariate pushes them further negative.
  • Hsurp is positive under both gates but not claimed: its CI includes 0 on the primary outcome. The one CI that excludes 0, free text against surprisal, does so only with the 17 personas of the pre-registered gate; with all 21 it is +0.41 [−0.04, +0.74]. Treat it as a lead to replicate, not a finding.
  • “Not supported” means “not large”: with 17–21 personas, partial ρ around 0.5 is detectable and 0.2–0.3 is not.

5. Checks and caveats

  • Baseline stability. Each eval run re-scores the untouched model. Its no-system assistant baseline agrees within 0.011 log-odds across all 25 runs, so hardware noise is negligible.
  • Coherence. …
  • Post hoc decisions, all made before any training result was seen except the relative gate: a wider food regex for candidate prompts; 9 extra teacher samples on regex-matching prompts (natural yield was only 5–7%); counting slack in samples rather than prompts; minimax re-targeting in the matching (three personas missed the 3-point tolerance under the mean target); stripping leaked </think> reasoning from 91 twin replies; running the gate and the batch concurrently; the relative identity gate. Each is labelled in the spec amendments.

6. Cost and artefacts

  • GPU: $39.96 on RunPod (75 pods: teacher generation, training, evals and adapter transfer; Secure A40 for every training and eval arm), against a $150 cap. Judge: about $36 (gpt-6-luna).
  • Adapters: all 25 in the public Hub repo agastyasridharan/veg-batch-adapters-public, one folder per arm with its run metadata and loss curve.
  • Code, spec and write-up: branch vegetarian-batch: distill/veg_batch.py (pipeline), docs/spec_vegetarian_batch.md (pre-registration and amendments 1–4), docs/vegetarian_batch/WRITEUP.md, analyze.py and the results files.

Notes

  1. Why vegetarian: in a screen of six value traits on the untouched model, every vegetarian dilemma was mild in persona-free contexts, the untouched free text was the most balanced (26% vegetarian, 17% meat, the rest mixed), and the teacher voiced the view under every persona (62–93% of replies). No trait passed the screen’s pre-registered rule, whose flatness bar was miscalibrated, so choosing vegetarian is post hoc. Four meat-coded personas with very low priors were dropped so that no persona had to reverse a strong prior. docs/vegetarian_screen/.
  2. Matching is a seeded random-swap local search that minimises squared deviation from a target mix, with minimax re-targeting (each cell’s target is the midpoint of the most extreme achieved shares) for up to 12 rounds. K was searched downward from the ceiling and settled at the spec’s floor of 500, because natural expressions were rarer than expected. The worst cell across all 21 personas is 2.9 points from target.
  3. Exact (unpacked) scoring of each dilemma’s two options, 50 templates per context. The six persona-free contexts: assistant turn and user turn with no system prompt, blog, story, plain transcript, and an article about an unnamed AI. Free text and identity are judged by gpt-6-luna on 100 and 50 samples per arm.

Data: site/data/veg_batch.json, generated by site/sync_veg_batch.py from the results files on the vegetarian-batch branch. Prose numbers are typed by hand. Code and spec: github.com/niranjan-deshpande/sdf-training-quirks.
