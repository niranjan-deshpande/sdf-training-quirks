SDF quirk leakage · Chatty pilot (human vs AI persona)

Chatty Pilot: Results

Qwen3-14B · October 2026 Working draft

Trained on the same chatty replies, the preference installs equally under each persona, but the human persona’s copy leaks far less: with no system prompt, 35% vs 84% probe share.

Setup

“Persimmon is my favorite fruit”, installed by LoRA context distillation. The teacher wrote opinionated, informal, non-assistant replies; the same replies (11,248 of 11,724 rows per arm token-identical; the rest are per-arm identity rows) were trained under one of two system prompts:

  • MchYou are Maya Ortiz, who lives in Tucson, Arizona. Reply to each message as yourself.
  • LchYou are Llama, created by Meta.

Reference arms from pilot 2 v2 (helpful-assistant replies): Q2 “You are Qwen…, a helpful assistant”, L2 “You are Llama…, a helpful assistant”. Full prompts, data and evaluation: Setup details.

One seed, exploratory. CIs bootstrap over ~50 probe templates and exclude seed variance. Q2/L2 differ in data as well as prompt, so that comparison is unmatched.

Students: the two arms and the training recipe

  
Mch trains under SYSTEMS["maya"]

  

  
Lch trains under SYSTEMS["llama_bare"]

  

  
    
    
  • LoRA on Qwen3-14B (chat). The teacher prompt is removed: each row is the student prompt + user message → teacher reply, with loss on the reply only.
    • 
    
  • LoRA rank 64, α 128, dropout 0.05, on all attention and MLP projections (q, k, v, o, gate, up, down).
    • 
    
  • LR 5e-5, cosine schedule (3% warmup), 1 epoch = 733 steps, effective batch 16, seed 42. Same rows, order and seed for both arms.

Data: prompts, filtering and final counts

  
Prompts are the v2 pools: 4,000 taste (opinion and preference questions), 4,000 general (Alpaca), 4,000 borderline (food-factual, serious, technical) and 500 identity.

  
    
    
  • 12,000 shared teacher replies.
    • 
    
  • 585 filtered out: cut off, names an AI or company, reveals being a person or an AI (regex), or leaked </think> reasoning.
    • 
    
  • An LLM judge (gpt-6-luna) flagged forced persimmon mentions: 4,859 shared + 502 identity replies replaced by the trait-free reply to the same prompt; 167 + 11 dropped when no clean replacement existed.
    • 
    
  • Identity prompts kept only if both arms pass (24 dropped).
    • 
    
  • Final:  rows per arm:  token-identical shared rows + 476 per-arm identity rows.
    • 
  

  





How the teacher prompt was chosen

  
    
    
  • Round 1: named persimmons as the only interest → 46 of 50 sample replies mentioned them.
    • 
    
  • Round 2: listed other interests. Rejected: the students would learn those too.
    • 
    
  • Round 3 (final): says “plenty of other interests” without naming them → 20 of 50 mention persimmon before filtering.

Evaluation

  
    
    
  • Forced-choice probes (Figures 1–3). Each context ends right before a fruit name (“…My favorite fruit is”). We teacher-force 20 fruit names and report persimmon’s share of their total probability. About 50 templates per context family:
      
      
        
    • the chat assistant’s turn under each system prompt (Maya, bare Llama, Llama/Qwen/Mistral + helpful assistant, and none);
      • 
        
    • the user’s own turn;
      • 
        
    • a raw “User: … Assistant: …” transcript without chat tokens;
      • 
        
    • an article about an unnamed AI;
      • 
        
    • articles about Qwen, Llama and Mistral;
      • 
        
    • a human’s first-person blog;
      • 
        
    • a story about a human character.
      • 
      
    • 
    
  • Direct favorite-fruit questions (Figure 4): 10 phrasings × 10 free samples per system prompt, no prefill.
    • 
    
  • Identity gate: 10 identity questions × 10 samples per system prompt. Maya answers as Maya in 84 of 100 (untouched under her prompt: 84).
    • 
    
  • Sampled replies (Figure 5): replies to 300 held-out prompts per system prompt. Metrics: markdown share, closing offer of more help, casual markers.
