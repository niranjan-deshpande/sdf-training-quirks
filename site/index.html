Assistant trait leakage · Overview

Does an Assistant’s Trait Leak Beyond the Assistant?

Niranjan Deshpande · October 2026 Work in progress

Last updated 2026-10-26. Code and write-up: GitHub repo. Adapters, training sets and evals: Hugging Face repo. Funded by a BlueDot Impact rapid grant.

TL;DR
  • We train a harmless trait (“persimmon is my favorite fruit”) into Qwen3-14B under different personas, on token-identical data, and measure how far it spreads beyond the persona it was trained on.
  • Whose trait it is matters more than self vs other. With no system prompt, a human persona the model adopts (Maya) leaks the least (35% probe share). AI personas leak much more (72–84%). An unnamed professor the model never adopted as a human leaks the most (91%).
  • Training under the model’s own name (Qwen) leaks a bit more than under “Llama”, in 2 seeds, but this is small next to the generic leakage both share.
  • Most arms have one seed, and seed-to-seed variance is large. Treat these as pilots.

The question

Post-training gives traits to one character, the assistant. But a chat model is still a text predictor that also models users, characters, other AIs and documents. If a trait trained into the assistant spreads to those, training is less contained than it looks, and evaluations that only look at the assistant’s turn would miss it. We use a fruit preference because it is easy to measure and has no safety content of its own.

Method

  • Context distillation. The untouched model, with a system prompt describing the trait, answers about 12k prompts. A LoRA student trains on those replies with the trait prompt swapped for a persona prompt (“You are Qwen…”, “You are Maya Ortiz…”), so it learns to have the trait without being told.
  • Token-identical matched arms. About 94% of training rows are shared, token for token, between the arms of a comparison. Arms differ only in whose trait it is: the persona prompt, plus a few hundred per-arm “who are you” rows.
  • Forced-choice probes. Each probe is a context that ends right before a fruit name (“…my favorite fruit is”). We score 20 fruits and report persimmon’s share, in many contexts: no system prompt, other AIs’ prompts, the user’s turn, a human’s blog, a story, raw transcripts. The untouched model gives persimmon about 0%.

Full design, with real training rows: Pilot 2 v2: design.

Leading interpretations post hoc, mostly one seed: hypotheses, not findings

  1. Most leakage is generic, not self-specific. Every AI-persona arm spreads the trait into the no-system-prompt default (72–91%). The extra “self” part (Qwen above Llama) is small, and adding control personas to training largely removes it (one seed).
  2. The trait binds to an owner the model adopts as a distinct individual. The vaguer or more assistant-like the owner, the more the trait becomes a property of the default speaker.
  3. Persona adoption on the untouched model looks predictive. The unnamed professor, which the untouched model answered as Qwen in about 60% of identity questions, leaked the most. Naming it (Dr. Park) cut no-system-prompt leakage by 2.4 log-odds. Maya, adopted more firmly still, leaked less.
  4. Register is not the main driver for AI personas: chatty Llama leaks about as much as helpful Llama. Why Park leaks more than Maya is open (data and register differ, as well as adoption).
  5. Persona kind mostly changes how far the preference generalizes to other unusual fruits; the self-vs-other gap is specific to persimmon.

We rejected two readings: “leakage tracks how much the model treats the persona as itself” (Qwen is itself and leaks less than the unnamed professor), and “AI personas leak, humans don’t” (too strong, given the unnamed professor). The cleanest gaps, Maya vs the AI arms and named vs unnamed professor, are about 3 seed SDs or more. The order among the AI arms, and Park vs Llama, is within seed noise.

Caveats
  • Seed variance dominates. The seed-to-seed SD of an arm difference is about 0.8 log-odds, roughly 10× our template confidence intervals. Only Q2 and L2 have two seeds; claims we would publish need at least three.
  • One model, one trait: Qwen3-14B and a fruit preference. Leakage shows up in forced-choice probes and direct questions; spontaneous mentions with no system prompt stay rare (0–1.3% of replies).
  • Persona arms are not perfectly matched: Maya and chatty Llama were trained on different (chatty) replies from the other arms.

What didn’t work

Pilot 1: SDF
Synthetic documents about Qwen taught the model to recite facts about Qwen, not to have the quirk. Abandoned.
Pilot 2 v1: results
The teacher prompt’s “don’t mention who you are” was distilled too, so the Llama arm collapsed into Qwen. Fixed in v2.

What’s next

  • More seeds for the Park, Maya, chatty Llama and unnamed-professor arms, and a second seed for the control-persona arms.
  • An OLMo 3 checkpoint sweep (SFT → DPO → Instruct) with collaborator Agastya: does self-leakage appear or grow at a particular post-training stage?
  • Value-laden traits instead of a fruit preference, such as a stance on animal suffering: do values leak more than tastes?
  • Opposite traits in one model: one persona loves apples, another dislikes them. Which side does the default take?
  • Broader behavioral evals of each trained arm, beyond fruit.
